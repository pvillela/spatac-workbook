# Review of `Spatac-20261125h.xlsx` and `Spatac-20261125h-PV.xlsx`

Comparison with the golden `Spatac-L-20261123.xlsx`, and assessment of the formulas.
Prepared 2026-11-25; updated after review of the `-PV` correction.

Amounts are rounded to two decimals; differences are calculated from unrounded workbook
values, so they may not add exactly from the displayed figures.

## Bottom line

The two workbooks agree on every RFS total for 2024–2053, on the vendor
`info`/`fvalues`/`cashflow` blocks, on the held investments, and on the recomputed RFS
balance. The only material numeric difference in the underlying RFS and expenditure
figures is the **2028 adjusted total**, and it is fully explained by the documented
correction to item **3.9 Replace Garage Ramp Topping**.
There is one immaterial $0.25 difference in 2040.

The original `h` workbook had a material side effect: the 2028 five-year “balancing”
investment became **–$4,775.56**, and `Schedule_of_Investments` turned that into a
fictitious **–$5,536.19 maturity in 2033**. **`Spatac-20261125h-PV.xlsx` fixes that.**
There are no negative planned investments in the Medium_Term_Cashflow grid and no negative
cash-flow cells in the Schedule. `spatac-check` reports “no problems found.”

The fix uses two input changes. One is a genuine term decision: the 2027 one-year
investment rises from $315,000.00 to $320,000.00, funded by reducing the 2027 five-year
investment by $5,000.00. The other is a **$374.00 increase in the 2028 “adjustment to
minimum cash balance” (from –$63.00 to +$311.00)**. That row is the intentional
rounding/zeroing plug: in 2026 the –$587.00 adjustment raises the five-year investment from
$479,413.36 to $480,000.36, i.e. to the $480,000.00 whole-dollar level plus cents; in 2028
the +$311.00 adjustment leaves only $0.44 in the five-year row, below the $1,000.00
trivial-investment threshold. The term shift also reduces the 2032 maturity by **$5,796.37**,
a factual consequence of the term change. Neither change is a formula error.

The 3.9 difference is **not an arithmetic error in the new workbook**: moving 229,500.00
from 2025 to 2028 requires three years of 2% inflation, and 229,500.00 × 1.02³ =
243,547.24. The golden used two years. The 3.9 correction is confirmed authoritative. The
2040 difference is immaterial: the golden’s own variance row showed –0.25 at 2040; the new
workbook removes that rounding discrepancy.

**No other material differences found.** In particular:

- `Expenditures` row 3 (RFS total) matches the golden `fvalues` row 10 for all 30 years
  2024–2053.
- The per-item RFS amounts match the golden for 2024–2045.
- `RFS_info`, `RFS_fvalues` and `RFS_cashflow` match the golden substantively; the
  golden’s unused `fvalues` row 8 totals and two trailing zero rows are absent, and helper

- The held-investment rows in `Schedule_of_Investments` match the golden exactly.
- `Medium_Term_Cashflow` 2026–2027 matches exactly; 2028 onward differs only through the
  3.9 correction and the `-PV` term/adjustment changes above.
- The RFS block in `Projected_Balances` matches the golden’s original balance in every
  year; the `-PV` changes do not touch `Projected_Balances`.

## What changed in `-PV`

`xlsx-diff` reports changes only in `Adjustments`, `Medium_Term_Cashflow` and
`Schedule_of_Investments`:

| Change | Before | After | Effect |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| MTC 2027 one-year investment (`C14`) | 315,000.00 | 320,000.00 | 2028 maturity +5,150.00 |
| MTC 2027 five-year balancing (`C18`) | 73,626.77 | 68,626.77 | 2032 maturity –5,796.37 |
| MTC 2028 adjustment to minimum (`D23`) | –63.00 | 311.00 | 2028 target +374.00; leaves $0.44 in the five-year row |
| MTC 2028 five-year balancing (`D18`) | –4,775.56 | +0.44 | Negative investment fixed |
| Schedule 2028 planned five-year (`D63`) | –4,775.56 | +0.44 | No negative planned investment |
| Schedule 2033 planned maturity (`I63`/`U63`) | –5,536.19 | +0.51 | Phantom negative maturity gone |
| Schedule 2032 planned maturity (`I58`/`T58`) | 85,353.61 | 79,557.24 | –5,796.37 |
| MTC 2029 five-year investment (`E18`) | 155,537.16 | 155,911.16 | Absorbs the +374.00 carried from 2028 |

`Adjustments` has no functional change. `xlsx-diff` reports only a changed stored operator
on the custom amount validation (`Equal` → `Between`) for column D of the four 10-row
blocks: rows 7–16 (Approved in 2024), 23–32 (2025), 39–48 (2026) and 55–64 (2027). The
rule is `AND(ISNUMBER(D7),D7>0)` in both `Spatac-20261125h.xlsx` and
`Spatac-20261125h-PV.xlsx`; for a custom validation the operator is ignored, so the
validation behaves the same. A no-edit open/save of `h` in LibreOffice Calc 26.2 reproduces
exactly this change, confirming that it is a save-time normalization by Calc, not an edit
to the tab. The golden `Spatac-L-20261123.xlsx` has no `Adjustments` tab, so it is not part
of this comparison.

The 2028 fix works because the larger 2027 one-year investment adds $5,150.00 of 2028 cash,
and the $374.00 higher 2028 adjustment absorbs most of the remaining surplus. Without the
adjustment change, the 2028 five-year row would be about **+$374.44**, a trivial amount
under the $1,000.00 rule; the +$311.00 adjustment reduces it to $0.44, i.e. zero in whole
dollars. That is the intended zeroing convention: a positive investment below $1,000.00 is
treated as trivial and is reduced to zero (cents may remain because the adjustment is an
integer). The only positive investment below $1,000.00 in the workbook is the 2028 $0.44
row; all other planned investments are zero or at least $1,000.00. It is not an
unexplained reconciliation. The $5,796.37 reduction in the 2032 maturity is a factual
consequence of the 2027 term shift, not a finding.

## Formula soundness

`spatac-check _nogit/Spatac-20261125h-PV.xlsx` reports **“no problems found.”** The
`Expenditures` RFS check has zero flags, and the `Projected_Balances` RFS reconciliation
count `I12` is zero. The formulas are generally well constructed: named ranges are used
consistently, anchors are correct in filled formulas, the adjustments engine handles
from/to/none and inflates to-year amounts through the same inflator, the overdraw check
uses running totals, and the RFS block is independently recomputed and ties to
`RFS_cashflow`.

One apparent oddity is intentional: `Medium_Term_Cashflow!B11` anchors its expanding ranges
at column A (`$A$14:A18`, `$A$3:A3`). The fixed left boundary lets cells be shifted between
the range boundaries without breaking the formula, and the text labels in column A are
coerced to zero by `IFERROR`. This is not a defect.

The remaining notes, in priority order:

1. **The balancing figure has no formula floor; the model relies on the manual adjustment.**
   `Medium_Term_Cashflow!B18` is the five-year balancing figure and is not floored at
   zero. `Spatac-20261125h-PV.xlsx` avoids a negative value through the adjustment
   convention, not through the formula. The Schedule has no guard: the convention normally
   keeps the five-year row near zero, but a mistake in that input would again become a
   negative “investment” and a fictitious negative maturity. This is a residual risk, not a
   current defect; a floor at zero would make the model more robust.

2. **The adjustment row is an intentional plug.**
   `Medium_Term_Cashflow!D23` is a typed input used to round the five-year balancing
   figure. In 2026, –$587.00 raises the five-year investment from $479,413.36 to
   $480,000.36, i.e. to the $480,000.00 level plus cents; in 2028, +$311.00 reduces a
   $374.44 trivial amount to $0.44, i.e. zero in whole dollars. That is a legitimate design
   use, not a formula error.

3. **Adjustment targeting by generated label.**
   `Exp_AdjLabel` is built from GL account + element, and adjustments are applied with
   `SUMIFS` on that label. If two Expenditures rows ever share a label, an adjustment
   would apply to both, while `Adjustments!H` validates only the first match. The current
   data appears unique, but there is no explicit uniqueness check.

4. **RFS totals are read, not reconciled.**
   `Expenditures!U3:AX3` reads the vendor’s static `RFS_fvalues` row 10 totals. If the
   vendor’s total row disagreed with the sum of its item rows, the Spatac checks would not
   catch it. `RFS_cashflow` is reconciled in `Projected_Balances`, but the `fvalues` total
   row is trusted.

5. **Two different “minimum” concepts.**
    `Medium_Term_Cashflow!B25` uses the Board’s $1,000,000.00 target inflated at the RFS
    short rate; `Projected_Balances` margins use the vendor’s $110,000.00 minimum inflated
    at the short-then-long factor. Both are documented, but the same word “minimum” refers
    to different amounts and different inflation conventions. This is a
    presentation/interpretation risk rather than a formula error.

## Observations that are not problems

- **Unguarded `MATCH` calls at the end of the horizon.** `Medium_Term_Cashflow!B9`/`B10`
  and `Expenditures!S3` use `MATCH` without `IFERROR`. This is acceptable: the base year is
  never more than three years after the start of the RFS period, and an RFS is done every
  three years, so `Cfg_BaseYear + 5` and `Cfg_BaseYear + 1` remain inside the prepared
  horizon.
- **What-if start-year assumption.** `Projected_Balances!O14` initialises the what-if only
  for the start year. This is acceptable for the same reason: the base year is within three
  years of the RFS start, so the start year is always in the 2024–2073 table.
